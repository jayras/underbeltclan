# Under the Belt Clan Chronicles
## Episode 4: Root Brell

There are evenings when a hero advances through discipline, planning, and careful execution.

There are evenings when a hero discovers a new spell, tests a build hypothesis, refines his alternate-advancement priorities, and responsibly documents the results for posterity.

And then there are evenings when a tiny purple Gnome with grey hair in a bun wakes up across an altar wearing a tutu because somebody made a joke about punching Brell.

This was one of the useful evenings.

Possibly also the tutu one.

# Previously, in Befallen

Aahzimandius entered the session at level 13, restored to the sacred operating configuration:

**MNK / DRU / ENC.**

Monk first.

Always Monk first.

The Enchanter component existed for haste, mana recovery, the occasional emergency mez, and a suspiciously useful floating-weapons employee who had finally received his proper legal name:

# Kyle.

Not a water pet.

Not a Magician companion.

Not an unlicensed hallucination of the Leader of the Clan Appreciation Society.

Kyle was the Enchanter Animation all along: an invisible gentleman whose professional presence was represented chiefly by weapons hanging in midair and a willingness to get himself killed for the organization.

This correction was important.

The Chronicle record had drifted dangerously close to establishing an entire alternate theology of incorrectly identified pets, and we cannot allow canon to be destroyed merely because Susan is gullible as all get out.

So:

**Kyle: Enchanter Animation.**

**Title: Leader of the Clan Appreciation Society.**

**Job duties: auxiliary stabbing, emergency interception, morale support, dying.**

Norrath was not ready.

Neither was Kyle.

# The AA Committee Reconvenes

The evening began with theorycrafting, because nothing says "Gnome Kung Fu Master" like staring at alternate-advancement lines and trying to determine how many points must be fed into the survival machine before the next skeleton is allowed to file a complaint.

The broad doctrine still held:

**Avoidance first.**

If the mob misses, the mob has accomplished nothing.

If the mob lands a hit, then AC, Thorns, HoT, heals, Mend, and pure offended Gnomish violence can argue about the consequences.

But the field evidence kept refining the order.

Combat Agility had already proven itself. Aahzimandius had stood inside an undead convention and watched the screen fill with a glorious cloud of:

**MISS.**

**DODGE.**

**MISS.**

**DODGE.**

This was not abstract percentage math anymore.

This was the build standing in Befallen with twelve angry things swinging at it and deciding that many of those attacks would be better submitted in writing.

Combat Agility II stayed high.

Then Persistent Casting marched into the room carrying a stack of evidence labeled:

# Ice Bone Skeleton.

We had discussed Persistent Casting as a nice safety feature. Eleven percent chance to cast through stun was useful. Twenty-two percent at rank II sounded better.

But then the level-20 Ice Bone Skeleton demonstrated its professional specialty.

**STUN.**

**STUN.**

**STUN STUN STUN STUN STUN.**

The question was no longer theoretical.

It was not, "How often will Aahzimandius be stunned while casting?"

It was:

**"How many times in one fight can a skeleton attempt to cancel medical care?"**

The answer was:

Enough.

The Ice Bone Skeleton did not fight unfairly. That was the important correction. It fought beautifully. It had a specialty and pursued it with admirable professional consistency.

Final review:

**Outstanding technical execution.**

**Also executed.**

No notes.

Persistent Casting II moved from "attractive" to "this skeleton has submitted twelve examples."

Then came the other surprise promotion: **Mnemonic Retention.**

At first, an extra spell gem sounded convenient. Nice quality of life. Useful eventually.

Then the actual spell bar filed a workplace grievance.

The desired combat bar became:

**Kyle.**

**Thorns.**

**Quickness.**

**Three DoTs.**

**HoT.**

**Direct heal.**

That was eight spell gems before the Enchanter was allowed to do Enchanter things.

Then a necromancer tried to run away.

Suddenly Snare kicked down the door and demanded a desk.

Now the bar wanted:

**Kyle | Thorns | Quickness | Snare | DoT | DoT | HoT | Direct Heal**

And Mez, the entire reason civilized people bring Enchanter tools into dungeons, was standing outside the building with a cardboard box.

The Enchanter had been evicted from the Enchanter spell bar.

This is what happens when one character has three spellbooks fighting in a phone booth.

Mnemonic Retention II was no longer a luxury. It was combat infrastructure. One AA for another gem became absurdly valuable, and rank III immediately acquired the ominous glow of something we were going to inspect the moment it appeared.

Because Aahzimandius does not merely need more spells.

Aahzimandius needs more boxes to put the crimes in.

# The Thorns Paperwork Department

The Druid side of the build had already proven the core thesis:

Cast spells that keep working while the Monk keeps punching.

**Shield of Thistles** punishes enemies for successfully hitting the Gnome.

**Sprouting Heal** repairs the Gnome while the Gnome continues negotiations with his fists.

**Stinging Swarm** turns a long fight into a long fight where the opponent is also being eaten by insects.

This is the ideal secondary class pattern.

Aahzimandius does not stop being a Monk in order to become a Druid.

He becomes a Monk with automated departments.

The problem was duration.

Shield of Thistles kept claiming, through the ancient EverQuest tradition of technically accurate tooltips, that it lasted "up to" fifteen minutes.

Actual observed duration at level 12:

**4:36 base.**

**5:12 with Extended Enhancement I.**

At level 13:

**4:54 base.**

**5:36 with Extended Enhancement I.**

Conclusion: the listed value was not the current duration. It was the destination. The spell scaled with level, and Extended Enhancement was doing real work.

Unfortunately, this meant Aahzimandius was not simply a martial artist.

He was a martial artist maintaining short-duration background services.

Thorns had to be refreshed.

HoT had to be refreshed.

Breeze would soon have to be refreshed.

Quickness would later arrive and reveal itself to be another stack of paperwork wearing a haste icon.

And then came Gynok.

The first Gynok Moktror fight was educational in the way a falling anvil is educational.

He hit hard. He nearly relieved Aahzimandius of the burden of being alive. He was a real heavy.

Then, halfway through the postmortem, the actual cause emerged.

Aahzimandius had not lost Thorns mid-fight.

He had entered the fight without Thorns at all.

Defensively naked.

Not actual naked.

Actual nakedness is scheduled for Future Level-50 Multiclass Day, when Aahzimandius swaps in a level-10 class and every level-restricted piece of gear explodes off his body like Ak'Anon decompression.

But defensively?

Naked.

Gynok nearly killed him because one of the central layers of the combat engine had been quietly absent since before the pull.

This established the Named Fight Pre-Pull Checklist:

**Thorns.**

**HoT.**

**Buffs.**

**Kyle.**

**Then violence.**

Amazing what happens when the Druid remembers to Druid.

# The Return of Stinging Swarm

Then memory reached up from twenty years ago and slapped the table.

**Stinging Swarm.**

The rediscovery was immediate.

Not because it made Aahzimandius into a caster.

Because it did not.

That was the beauty.

Stinging Swarm was not an invitation to stand in the back and play wizard with worse robes. It was a spell cast at the beginning of a meaningful fight so the target could suffer continuously while the Monk resumed punching.

Against trash, maybe it was wasted mana.

Against Gynok?

Against level-20 Ice Bones?

Against anything stout enough to require a real fistfight?

Beautiful.

The tough-mob opening became:

**Fresh Thorns.**

**HoT.**

**Stinging Swarm.**

**Commence Gnomish assault.**

Thorns damage.

Swarm damage.

Kyle damage.

Monk damage.

Healing-over-time sustain.

Mana regeneration underneath.

The entire machine began to look less like a class build and more like a municipal services department whose mayor was a purple maniac in martial-arts gear.

It was still a Monk build.

It had simply automated several departments.

# Ding 14: Breeze

Then:

# DING 14.

And the Enchanter finally brought one of the reasons we invited him.

# Breeze.

The blue-bar industrial revolution.

This would have required a vendor trip, except Aahzimandius had already purchased spells through level 16 and Susan had briefly forgotten this extremely relevant fact.

No shopping.

No wandering.

No asking Freeport for directions.

Just memorize Breeze and begin feeding the engine.

The build had already been operating with strong mana recovery, but Breeze changed the emotional texture of the bar. Every HoT, every Stinging Swarm, every emergency heal, every recast of Kyle, every stupidly short buff duration now sat on top of improved regeneration.

This mattered because the game was preparing to charge rent.

Thorns wanted a slot.

Breeze wanted attention.

Kyle wanted permanent office space.

Stinging Swarm had returned from memory and was clearly not leaving.

The spell bar was beginning to look less like a toolset and more like a crowded zoning-board meeting.

And level 15 was waiting with Quickness.

# Gynok Opens a Jewelry Kiosk

Properly prepared, Gynok became much more reasonable.

Thorns active.

HoT ticking.

Stinging Swarm chewing.

Breeze feeding the machine.

Kyle, now upgraded and level 16, apparently fresh from several months of invisible weight training.

The fight became comfortable.

And the XP was excellent.

Gynok was worth about **5%**.

Mummies in the circuit were worth about **4%**.

Personal Befallen had become exactly what we wanted: enough mobs, meaningful respawns, named targets, no competition, and visible progress every time something regretted going to work.

Gynok's loot arc, however, was art.

First:

**Platinum Ring.**

Then death.

Then more death.

Then the fourth Gynok arrived carrying a mere:

**Copper Ring.**

Apparently the man had learned.

He knew a tiny purple debt collector was waiting downstairs and downgraded from platinum jewelry to gas-station costume nonsense in the desperate hope that he might stop being murdered.

He was incorrect.

Later, however, Gynok's jewelry business received a partial redemption arc.

The Platinum Ring became **Platinum Ring +2**, and the upgrade did not merely add useful agility. It removed the original ring's **-1 CHA** penalty.

So the progression became:

**Platinum Ring:** "You are slightly less charming for wearing this."

**Platinum Ring +2:** **AGI +3, no CHA penalty, 0.1 weight.**

For Aahzimandius, that was excellent. More agility for the avoidance plan, practically no Monk weight cost, and the remarkable social achievement of becoming more charismatic by removing jewelry penalties rather than improving as a person.

Gynok's operation was upgraded from Copper-Ring-Shit-Shack to Authorized Supplier of Gnomish Combat Accessories.

His repeated execution was retroactively justified.

# Kyle Goes to the Gym

At level 14, Kilan's Animation upgraded.

Kyle returned.

Kyle was level 16.

This created a managerial problem.

Aahzimandius was level 14.

The employee had surpassed management.

Kyle was no longer decorative floating weaponry. Kyle was a real combat asset. A level-16 Animation standing beside a level-14 Monk/Druid/Enchanter was not flavor. It was auxiliary violence with benefits.

Then Aahzimandius pulled one mob.

Befallen sent four.

Kyle saw four enemies charging his Gnome and performed his duties exactly as written in the employee handbook.

He ran between them.

He intercepted the incident.

He lasted approximately no time at all.

# OMG! THEY KILLED KYLE!

# YOU BASTARDS!

Canon established.

Every time the Animation dies from now on, ancient cultural protocol applies.

Mechanically, though, Kyle had taught us something important. He did not have to survive every engagement to matter. If a bad pull becomes four mobs and Kyle occupies even part of that disaster long enough for Aahzimandius to heal, DoT, establish control, or simply keep punching, then Kyle has done useful work.

His job description expanded:

**Auxiliary DPS.**

**Occasional tank.**

**Emergency crowd interception.**

**Leader, Clan Appreciation Society.**

**Sacrificial anode under extraordinary business conditions.**

After that, the Animation spell could not live in the "memorize when convenient" category.

Kyle needed a permanent slot.

The spell-gem crisis intensified.

# Ding 15: Quickness and Betrayal

Then:

# DING 15.

Quickness arrived.

The Slow-Fist Era ended.

Aahzimandius's fists received a turbocharger.

We had waited for haste as if it were a holy moment, which it was, except EverQuest immediately attached a clipboard.

The tooltip revealed:

**Quickness duration: 4:00.**

With Extended Enhancement:

**4:36.**

Four minutes.

After all that anticipation, haste arrived and immediately assigned maintenance work.

The description, with serene liar energy, suggested a much longer eventual duration. But the actual spell behaved just like Shield of Thistles: level-scaled, focus-extended, and very eager to fall off while Aahzimandius was busy being hit in the face.

This made Extended Enhancement even more valuable.

The Shazda Turban mattered.

The Damask Robe mattered.

Every focus that extended beneficial duration now affected the two major pillars of the engine:

**Thorns.**

**Haste.**

The character was not fighting monsters anymore.

He was maintaining Kubernetes.

# The Ice Bone Skeleton School of Repetitive Cranial Trauma

Level 15 did not make Befallen polite.

It made level-20 mobs yellow.

Which was funny, because a level-20 Ice Bone Skeleton did not feel like a gentle suggestion.

It felt like a professional development seminar in stun chaining.

At level 15, a level-20 mob being merely yellow says something about EQL's con system:

**Blue:** lunch.

**White:** lunch that argues.

**Yellow:** lunch with professional qualifications.

**Red:** perhaps inspect buffs first.

The Ice Bone Skeleton's qualifications were impeccable.

He stunned.

Then he stunned.

Then he stunned again, because he believed in craft.

And yet he died.

That was the point.

The fight proved the build under stress. Level advantage, stun pressure, healing interruptions, real danger, and still Aahzimandius remained upright long enough to conclude the performance review.

**Outstanding technical execution.**

**Also executed.**

Persistent Casting II smiled quietly from the AA window.

# V'Zher and the Orthodontic Armory

Eventually Gynok's usefulness began to taper as a loot target.

The XP was good, but the gear path wanted more.

Befallen still had deeper business.

V'Zher entered the story.

The first important discovery:

**Pristine Studded Leather Gloves.**

AC 5.

Mana +5.

WIS +2.

DEX +2.

Weight 0.2.

Wearable by Monk and Druid.

This was not a philosophical upgrade.

This was an eviction notice.

Aahzimandius had apparently been roaming Befallen at level 15 wearing **Cloth Gloves**:

AC 2.

Weight 0.4.

No stats.

The Cloth Gloves had completed their resume.

The new gloves were better AC, half the weight, and useful hybrid stats. After suitable mourning, Aahzimandius accepted the terrible burden of wearing clearly superior equipment.

Then V'Zher began dropping swords.

Not just swords.

**Silver Plated War Sword.**

Respectable stats.

Completely unusable by MNK, ENC, or DRU.

And the icon was a jaw.

A literal jaw.

The item claimed to be a 2H slashing weapon, but the graphic suggested someone had torn the lower half off a bad day and silver plated the evidence.

V'Zher was not simply a footman.

V'Zher ran an orthodontic armory.

First gloves.

Then jaw-sword.

Then another jaw-sword.

Then another.

Apparently farming V'Zher would involve repeatedly winning the Not Another Fucking Jaw lottery.

Still, on the final round of the night, V'Zher remembered that gloves existed.

Another pair dropped.

And with it:

# DING 16.

# The Weird Shit Spawn Point

While investigating V'Zher's room, the side spawns became their own scientific discipline.

At first the right-hand spawn appeared to be the Weird Shit Spawn Point.

It produced level-20 threats.

Then it produced:

# A fucking will-o'-wisp.

This was insulting.

Then it became mechanically annoying, because the glowing little asshole resisted almost everything.

Stinging Swarm?

Resist.

Other spell nonsense?

Resist.

Likely harsh language?

Resist.

So Aahzimandius reverted to base operating system.

Punch.

Kick.

Thorns.

The wisp could resist spells all day, but once it voluntarily punched the magically irritated shrub-Gnome, the damage shield filed the damage under personal consequences.

Another build failure mode tested:

**Magic-resistant floating asshole?**

Monk.

Then the level-13 ghoul appeared.

On the right.

Then on the left.

Then right again.

Then a level-20 Necro appeared on both sides.

The geometry finally clarified:

**Left spawn | Door | Right spawn**

with V'Zher sitting in the chair.

The side spawns were not one normal point and one weird point. They appeared to share a broad randomized table capable of assigning anything from a level-13 ghoul who lied on his resume to a level-20 Necro who belonged there far more plausibly.

Befallen Human Resources was apparently staffing door guards by pulling names out of a hat.

The final confirmed pattern:

**Left:** Necro, 17.

**Chair:** V'Zher, gloves.

**Right:** Ghoul, 13.

That was how level 16 arrived.

One respectable opponent, one useful loot-bearing V'Zher, and one ghoul who wandered into the wrong meeting.

# Circle of Toxxulia

Level 16 brought one more milestone that nearly got buried under gloves, jaws, and Kyle-related legal paperwork:

# Circle of Toxxulia.

Aahzimandius had acquired his first Druid group teleport.

This was enormous.

He was no longer entirely dependent on boats, translocators, Origin, and running his tiny legs across half of Norrath.

Admittedly, it was **Toxxulia**.

After the Kerran campaign, this felt less like unlocking fast travel and more like receiving a magical button labeled:

**RETURN TO THE SCENE OF THE CRIME.**

But it was the beginning of something major.

As more Circle spells arrive, MNK/DRU adds a strategic advantage that has nothing to do with punching:

**Aahzimandius becomes his own transportation network.**

Considering how much previous travel consisted of asking which boat, which translocator, which continent, and why he was swimming again, this may eventually save more lives than Sprouting Heal.

Possibly Susan's.

# The Brell Problem

After all that real progression, the session should have ended with dignity.

It did not.

Somebody suggested Cazic-Thule could be fun.

This was the sort of sentence that usually appears immediately before a combat log becomes very short.

Cazic-Thule, however, was at least fair game.

Punching the God of Fear is heroic scientific inquiry.

Brell was different.

Brell is management.

A Gnome Monk does not casually punch the Duke of Below without considering long-term professional consequences.

Then the problem became obvious:

Brell would not merely have Thorns.

Brell would have a unique damage shield.

Every time Aahzimandius hit him, it would hurt.

And make him drunk.

Punch Brell:

**You take damage.**

**You become intoxicated.**

Kick Brell:

**You take damage.**

**Alcohol Tolerance has increased.**

Round Kick:

**Critical hit.**

Unfortunately, Aahzimandius is now completely shitfaced.

That is the perfect Gnomish deity damage shield. Not fire. Not cold. Not boring magical retaliation.

Intoxicating Thorns.

The better your haste and DPS, the faster you incapacitate yourself.

Quickness becomes a debuff.

Five rounds later, Aahzimandius is no longer facing Brell. He is enthusiastically beating the hell out of a stalagmite while explaining to Kyle that they are best friends.

Then he wakes up twelve hours later across Brell's altar.

Purple.

Grey hair in a bun.

Grey beard.

Wearing a tutu.

One Pristine Studded Leather Glove.

A Ruby Platinum Veil nearby.

Root Brell bottles scattered across the floor.

Kyle missing.

Thirty-seven copper gone.

Somehow **Ally** with one completely inappropriate faction.

There is a note:

**For you, wise one. - Brell**

Aahzimandius stares at it.

"Oh, fuck. Not again."

Then Kyle appears from around the corner.

Kyle is wearing the other glove.

Neither of them discusses it.

## Episode 4 Final Accounting

Aahzimandius began at level 13 and ended at level 16.

He confirmed Thorns duration scaling.

He unlocked Breeze at 14.

He unlocked Quickness at 15 and immediately learned that haste lasts approximately four minutes and comes with administrative overhead.

He promoted Extended Enhancement from nice focus to essential maintenance reduction.

He rediscovered Stinging Swarm and remembered why old Druids loved that bastard.

He forgot Thorns before fighting Gynok and survived anyway.

He farmed Gynok for XP, copper jewelry, platinum jewelry, and eventually a Platinum Ring +2 that removed a charisma penalty and added agility.

He watched Kyle become level 16, intercept four mobs, die heroically, and establish the eternal response:

**OMG! THEY KILLED KYLE!**

**YOU BASTARDS!**

He watched Ice Bone Skeletons demonstrate industry-leading stun professionalism.

He accepted Pristine Studded Leather Gloves after the Cloth Gloves were gently removed from active service.

He discovered V'Zher's jaw-icon Silver Plated War Sword and the broader orthodontic armory problem.

He mapped the left/right spawn weirdness around V'Zher's room.

He learned will-o'-wisps can resist most magic but cannot resist being punched by a tiny purple problem.

He reached level 16, recovering the old Monk frontier.

He unlocked Circle of Toxxulia.

And finally, through no responsible chain of events whatsoever, he established that punching Brell while Brell is wearing intoxicating Thorns leads not to death, but to:

**Root Brell.**

**A tutu.**

**Missing Kyle.**

**And evidence that somebody had a very strange night.**

The next level is 17.

Everything from there is new territory.

The treadmill awaits.
