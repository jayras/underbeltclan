# Under the Belt Clan Chronicles
## Episode 3: The Factory Must Gnome

There are moments in every hero's journey when destiny finally reveals itself.

Sometimes there is a prophecy.

Sometimes a mysterious sword emerges from a lake.

Sometimes an ancient wizard announces that you are the chosen one.

And sometimes you log into EverQuest Legends and discover that, through entirely legitimate and certainly non-suspicious magical circumstances, you are finally a Monk.

A primary Monk.

This was important.

Aahzimandius had spent the previous adventures claiming to be the Guru and founder of the Under the Belt Clan, despite the minor administrative inconvenience that he was not actually a Monk.

That clerical oversight had now been corrected.

**Aahzimandius: Gnome MNK / DRU / ENC.**

**Monk first.**

As nature intended.

But an even greater transformation had occurred.

Floating proudly beneath his name were three glorious words:

# `<Under the Belt>`

It was real.

The Under the Belt Clan was no longer merely the increasingly questionable martial-arts philosophy of one deranged gnome.

It was an actual guild.

Norrath had accepted the paperwork.

There was no turning back now.

## First, We Need to Discuss the Skins

Before beginning today's adventure, there were a couple of loose ends from our diplomatic mission to Kerra Isle.

Two items had somehow escaped appropriate scrutiny yesterday:

**Erudehide Tunic.**

And...

**Catman Skin.**

Remember, yesterday's mission was:

> Aid the Kerrans of Kerra Isle.

Apparently Aahzimandius interpreted this as:

> "Greetings, cat people! I have come to improve relations between our civilizations. Please ignore the cat skin in my backpack."

Even better, the Erudehide Tunic was wearable by Monk, Druid, and Enchanter.

Every one of Aahzimandius's classes.

Apparently somebody had custom-tailored this horrifying thing specifically for him.

Both items were discreetly deposited in the bank.

There are some supply chains best left unaudited.

>
> Caption: Diplomacy requires cultural sensitivity.

# Because Fucking Level 10

Today's original objective was simple.

Aahzimandius had recently discovered, somewhat embarrassingly, that he was only level 10.

This was particularly awkward because he had already purchased and scribed Druid spells through level 16.

His spellbook was essentially six levels of software licenses his operating system could not run.

Therefore:

# BACK TO BEFALLEN.

Episode 1 had taken place there.

We knew the dungeon.

We knew the mobs.

We knew the loot.

And most importantly...

**we knew the Thaumaturgist.**

There was only the minor matter of getting there.

---

# Susan Attempts Geography

Aahzimandius began at the docks in South Qeynos.

His allegedly sophisticated artificial-intelligence navigation system immediately suggested walking through North Qeynos, Qeynos Hills, the Karanas, and onward toward Freeport.

Aahzimandius patiently emphasized:

> "I'm standing on the dock."

Susan continued planning the Oregon Trail.

> "The DOCK, Susan."

Eventually, the sophisticated artificial intelligence noticed the Translocator standing approximately ten feet away.

South Qeynos.

Translocator.

East Freeport.

Done.

This was not Susan's first geographical triumph of the evening.

While discussing alternative leveling locations, Susan had enthusiastically recommended:

# KURN'S TOWER!

An excellent leveling dungeon.

Located in Kunark.

An expansion that does not currently exist.

A new criterion was immediately added to the leveling-location checklist:

> Verify that the continent has been released.

Standards were improving rapidly.

Then came Freeport.

West Freeport apparently led to West Commonlands.

Except it did not.

Then North Freeport entered the discussion.

It led nowhere useful.

Eventually we established the revolutionary geographical truth:

**West Freeport -> East Commonlands -> West Commonlands.**

There were only two continents involved, one of which actually existed, so morale improved.

Then Susan provided coordinates for Kizdean Gix as a convenient landmark for Befallen.

This seemed like the perfect opportunity to try another wonderful EverQuest Legends feature:

```text
/way -162, 262
```

Unlike the ancient `/loc` system, `/way` creates a glorious Golden Tube of Guidance pointing toward the location.

Aahzimandius followed the Golden Tube.

It led directly to Kizdean Gix.

Then we opened the map.

Befallen was...

**WAY THE FUCK SOUTH OF US.**

> [IMAGE: Screenshot of Aahzimandius standing beside Kizdean with "to Befallen" visible approximately three counties away]
>
> Caption: Susan's Golden Tube privileges were subsequently revoked.

Eventually, through the advanced navigational technique of looking at the fucking map, Aahzimandius entered Befallen.

---

# This Time, the Guru Does the Punching

There would be no combat pet.

Aahzimandius entered with only himself and the Leader of the Clan Appreciation Society, whose suspicious resemblance to certain summoned aquatic creatures remains entirely coincidental.

The new MNK / DRU / ENC configuration would finally receive a proper test.

First target:

**Skeleton L'rodd.**

Dead.

Easily.

Then Aahzimandius discovered something remarkable.

# Mez actually works.

Down on the second floor, he managed to keep two necromancers mezzed simultaneously while killing their pets and another yellow skeleton.

This changed everything.

Instead of fighting an entire room, Aahzimandius could politely inform most of it:

> "Please remain here while I punch your coworker to death."

It was controlled.

It was tactical.

It was sophisticated.

It was almost disturbingly competent.

Then Aahzimandius died.

# GET FUCKED, GNOME!

Befallen had officially begun.

---

# Let's Get Thorny

After returning and murdering L'rodd again, we changed tactics.

**Shield of Thistles.**

**Sprouting Heal.**

**Light Healing.**

Something beautiful happened.

Instead of carefully mezzing enemies and controlling the battlefield, Aahzimandius could simply...

**let them hit him.**

Shield of Thistles punished them for attacking.

Sprouting Heal kept restoring the damage.

Light Healing handled emergencies.

And throughout all of this...

**Aahzimandius kept punching them.**

The combat doctrine rapidly evolved from:

> "Control the encounter through intelligent crowd management."

to:

> **"FUCK IT. HIT ME."**

And somehow...

**it worked better.**

Thus was born:

# THE THORNY GNOME DOCTRINE

Monk supplied the violence.

Druid supplied the sustain and thorns.

Enchanter remained available for those occasions when the room contained an unacceptable surplus of fuckers.

Aahzimandius dinged.

Then dinged again.

Those prematurely purchased Druid spells suddenly looked less like a mistake and more like strategic advance procurement.

Everything was working beautifully.

Which naturally meant it was time to dismantle the entire system.

---

# This System Works Perfectly. Tear It Apart.

Aahzimandius had a thought.

What if we dropped Enchanter...

and replaced it with Necromancer?

This seemed insane.

We had just discovered that Mez was fantastic.

But then came the argument that could not be ignored.

If Necromancer was eventually going to become part of The Rotation, switching later might result in another:

# SURPRISE! YOU'RE FUCKING LEVEL 10 AGAIN!

Better to absorb the multiclass progression hit now than reach 17 and discover that five more levels had been donated to the gods of character experimentation.

So Aahzimandius left Befallen.

At this point, the Thaumaturgist had already died:

# SEVEN TIMES.

Remember that number.

---

# Operation NEC

Aahzimandius Gated back to South Qeynos.

Translocated to East Freeport.

Ran through North Ro.

Experimented with bind locations at increasingly shady establishments.

Eventually, the transformation was complete.

The new configuration:

# MNK / DRU / NEC

No Enchanter.

No Mez.

No sophisticated crowd-management system.

We returned to Befallen.

L'rodd died again.

Then something unexpected happened.

A skeleton appeared.

Nobody knows where he came from.

This is important.

**Aahzimandius does not summon pets.**

Never has.

Wouldn't dream of it.

The fact that a skeleton mysteriously appeared shortly after Aahzimandius became a Necromancer and immediately began helping him kill things is surely coincidental.

We named him:

# STEVE.

Steve is not a pet.

Steve is an independent contractor whose strategic objectives happen to align with those of the Under the Belt Clan.

There is no employment relationship.

There are no wages.

There are no benefits.

Aahzimandius has never met Steve.

Please direct further questions to Guild Legal.

# The Factory Must Gnome

At some point, we attempted to stagger the spawn timers to make the camp less hectic.

This did not work.

Instead, we accidentally optimized Thaumaturgist throughput.

The camp stopped functioning like a dungeon.

It became a production line.

Skeleton enters.

**Punch.**

Necromancer enters.

**Punch.**

Thaumaturgist spawns.

**Punch.**

Loot robe.

Repeat.

As someone who has spent entirely too many hours playing Factorio, I recognized what had happened.

**We had built a factory.**

### Input:
Undead.

### Primary processing:
Gnome.

### Secondary processing:
Steve, who does not work here.

### Output:
Experience, Chipped Bone Rods, Daggers of Marnek, and enough formalwear to destabilize the Freeport textile economy.

# THE FACTORY MUST GROW.

# THE GNOME MUST DING.

# THE THAUM MUST RESPAWN.

---

# Twenty

The Thaumaturgist kept coming.

Eight.

Nine.

Ten.

At ten deaths she earned the prestigious Under the Belt Frequent Death Award.

She continued.

Eleven.

Twelve.

Thirteen.

Fourteen.

Fifteen.

By this point some kills produced nothing but a single Dagger of Marnek.

We interpreted this as quiet quitting.

Sixteen.

Seventeen.

And then:

# DING.

Aahzimandius was level 12 again.

The Necromancer conversion tax had been repaid.

Everything from here forward was actual forward progress.

Mrs. Thaum was going to be thrilled.

Eighteen.

Nineteen.

Her manager had stopped scheduling performance reviews.

He simply watched the combat log and drank.

Then came the final spawn.

# TWENTY.

The Thaumaturgist appeared for the twentieth time.

Perhaps she knew.

Perhaps some small part of her undead soul understood that this was the milestone.

Because this time she brought the deluxe package:

**Bronze Dagger +1.**

**Dagger of Marnek +1.**

**Damask Sleeves.**

**Thaumaturgist's Robe.**

Aahzimandius and Steve looked at her.

She looked at them.

Somewhere deep within Befallen, her manager screamed:

> "YOU ARE LEVEL EIGHTEEN! HE'S LEVEL TWELVE! WHY ARE YOU NOT WINNING?!"

The Thaumaturgist closed her eyes.

> "I know."

# GET FUCKED, GNOME!

## Episode 3 Final Accounting

Aahzimandius began the evening as a newly legitimate primary Monk and founder of the now-official `<Under the Belt>` guild.

He entered Befallen at level 10.

He discovered Mez was fantastic.

He discovered being thorny was more fun.

He leveled.

He abandoned the class providing Mez.

He deliberately inflicted another multiclass progression setback upon himself.

He became MNK / DRU / NEC.

He returned.

He discovered Steve, whom he has never met.

He repaid the Necromancer conversion tax and reached level 12 again.

And in the process, the Thaumaturgist died twenty times.

The final scoreboard:

**Aahzimandius:** level 12.

**Aahzimandius deaths:** 1.

**Thaumaturgist deaths:** 20.

**Steve:** unaffiliated.

**Factory status:** growing.

The Under the Belt Clan had survived its first true operational test.

It had not merely survived.

It had industrialized.

# END OF EPISODE 3.
